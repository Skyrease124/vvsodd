About PricePocketAsset

PricePocketAsset is an educational website that helps people understand and research tax lien and tax sale investing in all 50 states and DC, before they spend any money.

What you can do for free

  • Explore the map. Tap any state to see whether it sells tax liens, tax deeds or redeemable deeds, the interest rate or penalty, the redemption period, how bidding works, and who the governor is.
  • Read plain-language state guides with sources. Where sources disagree, we mark the figure verify.
  • Find county pages and parcel searches. We link to each state’s county sites, including all 67 Florida counties, and to official parcel searches so you can see a lot on a county map.
  • Browse real lien lists from Palm Beach County (Florida), Larimer County (Colorado) and Pinal County (Arizona), copied from official county files, with lot maps, county values and filters. Owner names are left out.
  • Use the calculators to work out interest and returns.
  • Keep a private account. Save liens you like and track the liens you bought, with that state’s terms and your redemption dates.

What members get ($5.50 a week)

  • AI question box. Ask about a state’s rules and get a short, sourced answer that uses only the information on this site.
  • AI lien screener. Ranks county liens with fixed, public rules (county value compared with tax owed, property type, size, and red and green flags) and explains each result in plain words, so you know which liens deserve a closer look.

What PricePocketAsset is not

PricePocketAsset gives education and research tools only. It is not legal, tax or investment advice. We do not sell liens, hold your money, or tell you to buy or bid on anything. Tax lien investing involves risk, including loss of principal. Rules change by county, so always verify everything with the county before you bid.

Who it’s for

Beginners and self-directed investors who want to learn how tax liens work, compare states, and shortlist liens to research further.

Where our information comes from

State rules come from statutes and investor-education sources, checked against each other. County lists come straight from official county files, and each lien shows its source and the date we checked. If we cannot confirm something, we say so instead of guessing.
