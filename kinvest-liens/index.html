How tax lien investing works

1

Owner falls behind

A property owner doesn't pay property taxes. The county needs that revenue.

2

Lien is auctioned

The county sells the unpaid-tax lien to investors, who pay the back taxes.

3

Owner redeems

The owner repays the taxes plus statutory interest during the redemption period. You earn that interest.

4

Or foreclosure

If never redeemed, some states let the lienholder start foreclosure. This is rare, and rules vary widely.

FAQ

Is tax lien investing risk-free?

No. Risks include a worthless or contaminated property, bankruptcy of the owner, lost redemption, and fees. Do your due diligence on every parcel.

Will I end up owning the property?

Rarely. Most liens are redeemed. Foreclosure is possible in some states and is a legal process.

How much money do I need?

Many liens are a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, though some counties require registration deposits.

Is this financial advice?

No. Kinvest Liens provides education only. Consult a licensed professional before investing.
